    The 20 Investment Principles¶

    A systematic approach to stock selection and portfolio management.

    🔍 Research & Analysis Principles¶

    1. Ignore the crowd¶

    You make money by going against the consensus.

    Application: Look for stocks with low analyst coverage, negative sentiment, or recent sell-offs where fundamentals remain strong.

    2. Always pick stocks where you feel you have a research edge¶

    Your advantage comes from knowing something others don't.

    Application: Focus on industries you understand deeply, companies you've researched extensively, or situations where your expertise gives you an advantage.

    4. Don't listen to management - They are paid to be bullish¶

    Management has inherent conflicts of interest.

    Application: Focus on third-party data, customer reviews, competitor analysis, and independent research rather than management guidance.

    5. Study competitors, suppliers, and customer behavior - Be a product junkie¶

    The ecosystem tells the real story.

    Application: Use the product, read customer reviews, analyze supplier relationships, track competitor moves, monitor industry dynamics.

    15. When investing in growth stocks always look for a controversy ("fight")¶

    Controversy creates opportunity.

    Application: Seek stocks with temporary headwinds, regulatory concerns, or market misunderstandings that create temporary price dislocations.

    📈 Quality & Growth Assessment¶

    3. You rarely go wrong investing in the company with the best product¶

    Product superiority drives long-term success.

    Application: Identify clear product leaders, assess competitive moats, evaluate customer loyalty and switching costs.

    9. The highest quality of growth is unit growth, then pricing, then margin expansion, then cash reinvestment¶

    Growth quality hierarchy matters more than growth rate.

    Application: Prioritize companies growing through increased unit sales over those relying on price increases or margin expansion alone.

    10. Be wary of companies that grow by buying other companies¶

    Acquisition-driven growth is often unsustainable.

    Application: Favor organic growth over M&A growth. If a company does acquire, ensure they have a strong track record of successful integrations.

    13. Two big value creators are brand extension and TAM expansion stories¶

    The best growth comes from expanding addressable markets.

    Application: Look for companies successfully extending their brand into new categories or expanding their total addressable market.

    14. High P/Es are a function of high future growth rates, and not the industry¶

    Valuation should reflect individual company prospects, not sector averages.

    Application: Don't avoid high P/E stocks if growth prospects justify the valuation. Focus on PEG ratios and growth sustainability.

    💰 Valuation & Timing¶

    6. Have in your mind what you think a stock is worth, which is different from price¶

    Intrinsic value vs. market price.

    Application: Develop DCF models, comparable company analysis, and sum-of-parts valuations to determine your price target independent of current market price.

    11. Sell discipline is selling a stock once it hits your price target, or your investment thesis changes¶

    Clear exit criteria prevent emotional decisions.

    Application: Set price targets and thesis checkpoints before buying. Review quarterly and sell when criteria are met.

    12. Short stocks that have bad businesses, and not because they trade at high P/Es¶

    Short based on business quality, not valuation alone.

    Application: Focus on companies with declining competitive positions, secular headwinds, or unsustainable business models rather than just expensive valuations.

    19. Stock buybacks are accretive if the E/P ratio exceeds the after tax cost of debt or return on cash¶

    Buybacks should create value, not just boost EPS.

    Application: Calculate whether buybacks make financial sense. E/P ratio should exceed cost of capital for buybacks to be value-creating.

    🎯 Strategic Positioning¶

    16. Buy stocks that can leverage key secular megatrends, and avoid those that will be hurt by them¶

    Align with unstoppable forces.

    Application: Identify major secular trends (AI, aging population, climate change) and find companies positioned to benefit while avoiding those that will be disrupted.

    17. Always consider cannibalization of existing products when sizing up new product opportunities¶

    New products may hurt existing revenue.

    Application: When evaluating new product launches, assess potential cannibalization of existing products and net revenue impact.

    18. Be wary of "hockey stick" sales forecasts absent new products or expansion to new distribution channels¶

    Growth must have a catalyst.

    Application: Scrutinize management projections for realistic drivers. Sustainable growth requires new products, markets, or distribution channels.

    20. Price cuts rarely add value since competitors usually match them¶

    Pricing power is crucial.

    Application: Favor companies with pricing power and differentiated products over those competing primarily on price.

    🎪 Investment Thesis Framework¶

    7. Be able to articulate in one sentence why you own a stock¶

    Clarity of thesis is essential.

    Application: Before buying, write one sentence explaining your investment thesis. If you can't, don't buy.

    Template: "I own [STOCK] because [CATALYST] will drive [METRIC] leading to [OUTCOME] over [TIMEFRAME]."

    8. Develop specific downside scenarios that would cause you to sell the stock¶

    Risk management through scenario planning.

    Application: Before buying, identify 3-5 specific scenarios that would invalidate your thesis and trigger a sale.

    📊 Principle Scoring System¶

    Rate each stock from 1-5 on each applicable principle:

    • 5 = Strongly Positive - Principle clearly supports the investment
    • 4 = Positive - Principle somewhat supports the investment
    • 3 = Neutral - Principle neither supports nor contradicts
    • 2 = Negative - Principle somewhat contradicts the investment
    • 1 = Strongly Negative - Principle clearly contradicts the investment

    Minimum Score for Purchase: 65/100 (average of 3.25/5 across all principles) Sell Trigger: Score drops below 50/100 or any principle scores 1/5
